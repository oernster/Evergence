# Plural Rooting, Routing and Shared Works

[← Evergence](../README.md) · [Architects](../ARCHITECTS.md) · [Memory](../MEMORY.md) · [Research](../RESEARCH.md) · [IUL and Mars](../iul-mars/README.md)

> **Status: provisional research extension.** The principles are proposed normative commitments; the graph is a sketch of a model, not a discovered law of history; the historical examples are illustrations with their sources cited, chosen for what they separate rather than for what they prove.

**Continuity does not require subordination. Independence does not require forgetting. Cooperation does not require a single root.**

## Why this is missing from the framework

Evergence seeks civilisational continuity and open-ended evolution without permanent stewardship ([VISION.md](../VISION.md); [ARCHITECTS.md](../ARCHITECTS.md) makes the architect dispensable by design). What the framework has not yet said is how a population or institution establishes a **new root of legitimate authority** while keeping its ancestry, its relationships and its capacity to work with the people it came from.

A civilisation may branch. The branch may contest inherited authority, gain independence or negotiate autonomy. None of those events should oblige it to erase its history or to sever every future connection; nor is independence, on its own, evidence that the new institutions are just. The extension therefore keeps four things apart that are often collapsed into one: **historical origin**, **present authority**, **ongoing relationships** and **shared achievements**.

## Proposed principle: sovereign cooperation and shared achievement

> Independent communities may keep shared histories, maintain or renew relationships and make new things together without accepting a common sovereign. Past conflict is evidence to preserve, not an instruction that later generations remain adversaries. Cooperation is voluntary, accountable and attentive to every community materially affected, including people who were excluded from the original agreements.

The organising statement: **independence permits divergence; friendship permits convergence; neither should require surrendering the other.** This complements the framework's commitments to preserved evidence, reviewable power, individual autonomy and the architect's eventual dispensability; it replaces none of them.

## Four words: root, rout, route, tour

The vocabulary starts from a deliberately playful group of English and French words. The play is a mnemonic. It is not etymology, not a linguistic lineage and not a theorem; the glossary says so.

| Word | Literal sense used here | Architectural analogy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Root** | An origin or a foundational authority (including the administrator account of a computer) | A source of identity, provenance or scoped legitimacy |
| **Rout** | The defeat or displacement of a force | A contest, disruption or displacement of an established authority arrangement |
| **Route** | A path or connection | A pathway for trade, diplomacy, learning, travel, cooperation or delegated operation |
| **Tour** | French *tour*, a tower; English *tour*, a journey | An emblem of exploration, exchange and the making of enduring works |

*Rout* and *tour* are anagrams: the same four letters, with the first and last exchanged around a fixed *ou*. They are not letter-by-letter reversals of each other. *Root*, *rout* and *route* resemble one another in sound and spelling, which is all that is claimed for them.

The progression **root → rout → route → tour** can be remembered as a narrative: an origin, a displacement, a path, a work. It is not a necessary sequence. Independence can be negotiated without any rout; a rout can be purely destructive; no monument is needed for reconciliation. Use it as an aid to memory, never as a law.

One further term carries the weight of the extension. **Re-rooting** is the act by which an entity establishes (or has recognised) a new foundation for its decision-making authority while keeping authenticated knowledge of its earlier roots. The second half of that sentence is the point.

### The computer analogy and where it stops

A privileged `root` login illustrates operational authority well. It also shows the limit of the analogy: creating several privileged accounts does not create several independent sovereigns, because every one of them controls the same machine inside the same trust boundary. What this extension proposes is **plural, scoped and contestable roots**, each defined by its domain, mandate, duration, constituency, accountability and means of revocation. A universal superuser with many passwords is the opposite of that. No technical metaphor may be allowed to hide consent or rights.

## Historical illustrations, each kept to what it shows

Dates and attributions below were checked against the institutional sources listed at the foot. Each example is chosen because it separates two things that a simpler story would merge.

### Boston, 1773; the Declaration, 1776; Paris, 1783

On the night of 16 December 1773 protesters in Boston destroyed 342 chests of East India Company tea in opposition to imperial policy. That was an act of resistance, not the founding of a state. The Declaration of Independence followed in 1776. Britain accepted American independence in the preliminary articles of 30 November 1782 and the formal Treaty of Paris was signed on 3 September 1783. Three phases, three years apart at each step: a challenge to an inherited claim, an assertion of a new root and its external recognition. Collapsing them into one moment is the first error the model is built to refuse.

*Ethical limit.* The new root did not deliver equal liberty to everyone inside its claimed boundaries. The histories and rights of Indigenous nations, of enslaved people, of women and of other excluded groups stay in the record. A change of root is not a universal emancipation; the model must be able to say so.

### The later British-American relationship

The two sovereigns later built a close strategic partnership, above all in the twentieth century. It was not an uninterrupted alliance from 1783: the War of 1812 was fought between them; there were later disagreements. The example shows that ending an authority edge neither erases historical connection nor forbids new voluntary routes, diplomatic, scientific or institutional, opened long afterwards.

### The Entente Cordiale, 1904

Britain and France signed the Entente Cordiale at London on 8 April 1904, turning a long rivalry towards cooperation. Its declarations settled colonial questions concerning Egypt and Morocco, with Spain's interests on the Moroccan coast also provided for. The populations of those territories were not parties to it. Two independently rooted entities opened a route without merging their sovereignty; and an agreement between two governments is not sufficient when third parties bear its consequences. The model records both facts, not only the first.

### The Statue of Liberty, 1886

The Statue of Liberty, dedicated in New York on 28 October 1886, was a gift of friendship from France to the United States. Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi was its sculptor; Gustave Eiffel designed the iron framework inside it, taking over the internal structure after Viollet-le-Duc's death and completing it in 1883; the pedestal was the American side's contribution, designed by the architect Richard Morris Hunt. Independent participants produced one shared work without surrendering their sovereignty; credit belongs to the people who did the work, not to a single national author.

### The Eiffel Tower, 1889

The Eiffel Tower was built by Eiffel's company between 1887 and 1889 as the centrepiece of the 1889 Exposition Universelle in Paris; its design came from his engineers Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier, with Stephen Sauvestre's decorative work. The engineering lineage that runs through Eiffel's company connects it to the Statue's framework. The Tower was **not** a reciprocal American gift to France and was not built as the companion half of an exchange of monuments; no source describes it as a gift from anyone. The complement is conceptual: related expertise contributed to two extraordinary works on opposite sides of the Atlantic. The French *tour* and the *rout* / *tour* letter swap are a mnemonic for moving from displacement to building; they are not history and not etymology.

## A formal sketch: many roots, several kinds of edge

This is a provisional representation, offered so that the distinctions above can be argued about precisely. It is not a law of history.

Let the civilisational record at time $t$ be a typed, directed graph

$$
G_t=(V_t,\;E_t^H,\;E_t^A,\;E_t^R,\;E_t^W,\;\mathcal T_t)
$$

with $V_t$ the entities (communities, institutions, states, projects, populations); $E_t^H$ ancestry, influence and provenance; $E_t^A$ authority claims and delegations, each bounded by a stated jurisdiction and scope; $E_t^R$ routes (diplomatic, cultural, commercial, scientific, communicative), each carrying its consent and status; $E_t^W$ contributions to shared works, with contributors and beneficiaries named; $\mathcal T_t$ the recorded transitions, disputes, recognitions and corrections.

An authority edge is not a bare arrow. At minimum it is

$$
a=(\text{issuer},\ \text{subject},\ \text{scope},\ \text{basis},\ \text{start},\ \text{end},\ \text{status}),
$$

where *status* ranges over asserted, contested, provisionally exercised, recognised, expired and revoked. **A claim's presence in the graph is never proof of its legitimacy.** A route records who proposed it, who agreed, who may revoke it, which populations it affects and whether they were represented. An ancestry edge confers no governing authority by itself:

$$
\text{ancestry}(u,v)\;\not\Rightarrow\;\text{authority}(u,v,t).
$$

### Re-rooting as a transition

Let $A_t$ be the set of authority relationships active at $t$. A stylised re-rooting is

$$
A_{t+1}=\bigl(A_t\setminus\lbrace a_{\text{old}}\rbrace \bigr)\cup\lbrace a_{\text{new}}\rbrace ,
$$

a transition inside a chosen model of active authority, not an instant resolution of a contested sovereignty in the world; competing claims and their evidence may coexist in the record long after effective authority has changed. Alongside it the archive $H_t$ satisfies the preservation aspiration of [MEMORY.md](../MEMORY.md):

$$
H_t\hookrightarrow H_{t+1},
$$

so that the earlier authority, the transition and the dissent remain recoverable. A legitimate re-rooting changes the first relation, possibly a great deal, while leaving the second intact. The 1773 to 1783 sequence is three such transitions, each with its own evidence.

### Shared works

A shared achievement $w$ has many contributors in distinguishable roles:

$$
w=F(c_1,\ldots,c_n;\ r,\ h,\ \kappa),
$$

with $c_i$ the contributions, $r$ the active relationships, $h$ inherited knowledge and $\kappa$ material and institutional constraints. $F$ names a possible model, not a known formula for cultural achievement. The record it demands: the collaborators, the labour that was overlooked, the communities affected and the differences among ownership, stewardship and significance, with room for disputed attribution. The Statue, with its sculptor, its engineer and its pedestal architect, is the worked example.

### Relation to the IUL discipline

For a quantitative state variable with a defined rate, $x_i(t)=\int f_i(t)\,dt+C_i$; distinct branches may carry distinct constants and distinct later trajectories. That is an analogy only. An integration constant grants no sovereignty and confers no legitimacy. Where the [IUL discipline](../iul-mars/README.md) applies, its rule holds: constants are kept and fixed only by a stated condition. There is no need to manufacture an integral for every historical event.

## Properties any implementation must keep

- **Plurality:** no required single origin, ruler, interpretation or permanent architect.
- **Scoped powers:** administrative authority bounded by jurisdiction and purpose, never an unlimited `root`.
- **Reversibility where possible:** delegation, federation, withdrawal, succession, peaceful independence and reconciliation each representable as a distinct transition.
- **Historical integrity:** provenance, competing claims, original documents, corrections and later interpretation stored separately ([MEMORY.md](../MEMORY.md)).
- **Representation:** the consent and rights of groups affected by decisions taken without them examined every time.
- **Rights and non-domination:** independence for one group justifies no coercion or exclusion of another ([ETHICS.md](../ETHICS.md)).
- **Reviewability:** challenges to authority go through fair procedure and independent assessment; success in conflict is not proof of legitimacy ([governance-integrity](../governance-integrity/README.md)).
- **Voluntary cooperation:** alliances, friendships and shared projects formed, revised or ended without erasing separate identities.
- **Continuity of knowledge:** a technique or work may have roots in many communities; provenance and credit follow it.
- **Architect restraint:** communities proceed without needing direction or approval from any central Evergence architect.

## A prospective case: Earth and Mars

Consider a settlement on Mars built with Earth-based scientific, financial and technical contributions, whose inhabitants later form institutions suited to their own circumstances. Earth and Mars could keep historical, familial, scientific and artistic links while negotiating how each governs itself. The model must assume neither that Earth holds perpetual sovereignty because it came first nor that Mars may ignore every prior commitment and the rights of affected parties. Legitimate governance there would turn on the applicable legal framework, standing obligations, the inhabitants' wishes, resource stewardship, safety and planetary protection.

Under the architecture above: Earth-to-Mars heritage is an $E^H$ edge; habitat, power, communications and atmospheric-engineering agreements are scoped $E^R$ edges; political claims and legal status are kept apart from scientific data and engineering authority; collaboration and shared works remain possible across different governance arrangements; and future Martian sovereignty is an unresolved legal and ethical research question, not a statement of current law. This is a governance extension beside [iul-mars](../iul-mars/README.md); nothing here alters its mass, energy or atmospheric calculations; no physics follows from a governance analogy.

## Tests the model must pass and how it fails

1. **Peaceful autonomy.** Two entities negotiate separate authority with no rout while provenance survives.
2. **Contested independence.** Asserted sovereignty, exercised control and later recognition are recorded as distinct events, without a pretence that consensus existed earlier.
3. **Third-party rights.** An agreement between major powers that harmed or excluded other populations is represented with the competing claims and any remedies.
4. **Institutional abuse.** An authority root that refuses review, rewrites history or claims every cooperation route is detectable as such.
5. **Conflicting roots.** Several valid identity or cultural roots coexist with one or more jurisdiction-dependent authorities.
6. **Relationship recovery.** A former adversary becomes a voluntary partner while neither erases the evidence of the earlier conflict.
7. **Shared-work attribution.** Contributions, ownership, maintenance, forgotten labour and cross-border reuse are documented without a single-author assumption.
8. **Failure of cooperation.** A relationship expires, is contested or fails without erasing earlier agreements or either party's independent legitimacy.
9. **Independent audit.** A reviewer can reconstruct who claimed authority, when, on what evidence, over whom and with what possibility of challenge.
10. **Mars analogue.** Heritage, technical control and political legitimacy stay distinct where Earth-based institutions build infrastructure later used by Martian communities.

**Failure conditions.** The model fails if it treats ancestry as authority, erases dissent, presumes that agreement between two governments speaks for everyone affected or labels an event "independence" before independence occurred. Any of these is grounds to revise the model, not the record.
