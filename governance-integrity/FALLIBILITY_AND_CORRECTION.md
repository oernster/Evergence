# Architectural Fallibility and Correction

[← Index](README.md) · [Reciprocal oversight →](RECIPROCAL_OVERSIGHT.md) · [Existing architects framework](../ARCHITECTS.md)

## Candidate fallibility principle (normative)

No architect, governing institution, scientific theory or protective mandate shall be presumed infallible. A system's ability to detect, admit, challenge and repair its own errors is part of its legitimacy. Confidence, sincerity, expertise and longevity do not confer exemption from correction.

The objective is not error-free government but government capable of **learning without requiring the continued permission or presence of its founding authority**.

## Operational requirements (design proposals)

- **Separability:** the goals of civilisational continuity must survive the replacement or removal of particular officeholders and institutions.
- **Error visibility:** publish decisions, assumptions, limitations, conflicts of interest, dissenting views and observable results, subject to narrowly justified privacy and safety protections.
- **Independent challenge:** affected parties and qualified independent reviewers can initiate scrutiny, not merely accept an invitation from the original decision-maker.
- **Correction pathways:** decisions should have explicit mechanisms for suspension, appeal, rollback, remedy, compensation where appropriate and institutional redesign.
- **Review clocks:** provisional rules, especially extraordinary powers, require expiry or affirmative independent renewal.
- **Record fidelity:** errors, failed interventions and corrections remain in the historical archive. Public-facing summaries may be updated but must not silently replace the source record.
- **Distribution of competence:** maintain alternative expertise and succession paths so that correcting a founder does not collapse the institution.

## A practical correction cycle

1. **Notice:** record a credible discrepancy between expected and actual results; permit reporting from outside the institution.
2. **Protect:** stop or narrow ongoing harm where possible before waiting for final attribution of blame.
3. **Investigate:** preserve evidence, establish provenance and test competing explanations.
4. **Decide:** select a proportionate correction through a process with meaningful independence, review and appeal.
5. **Repair:** reverse or mitigate the outcome where feasible; offer remedies to affected parties.
6. **Learn:** document what failed, how incentives contributed, what changed and when success or failure will next be assessed.

## Preventing self-certification

Internal conscience and institutional audits are useful but cannot themselves settle questions of legitimacy. Reviewers also err; therefore oversight should be **plural, overlapping and contestable**, with no institution capable of suppressing every independent account of its actions. This is an institutional design argument, not a mathematical deduction from Gödel's incompleteness theorems.

## Failure indicators (hypotheses)

Warning signs include self-exemption from published rules, retaliation against principled dissent, unexplained emergency extensions, undisclosed conflicts, uncorrected known harm, exclusive control of evidence or refusal to disclose testable assumptions. These signs warrant examination, not automatic conclusions of wrongdoing.

## What would disconfirm the proposed approach?

If a correction mechanism repeatedly increases harm, merely shifts authority into an equally unaccountable body or prevents timely protection in emergencies, it must be redesigned. Document the counterexample, not merely the intended benefit.
