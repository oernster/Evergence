# Research Programme: Test Protective Governance Against Its Own Failures

[← Index](README.md) · [Evidence and time](EVIDENCE_AND_TIME.md) · [Evergence research](../RESEARCH.md)

> **Status:** proposed evaluative programme. The following measures are research candidates, not validated measures of human moral worth, institutional virtue or civilisational success.

## Primary research questions

1. Can independently reviewable correction pathways detect and resolve damaging institutional decisions faster than internally controlled reviews, without disabling essential services?
2. Which distributions of oversight power resist collusion and capture under adverse incentives?
3. When does timely, proportionate intervention reduce harm compared with delayed, escalated intervention?
4. Can transparent, legally constrained deterrence reduce aggressive conduct without producing coercive institutional culture?
5. Do structured tragic-choice assessments identify more feasible options and avoid unjustified harm compared with binary emergency narratives?
6. Can an archive preserve contested evidence and corrections across changes of authority without creating a monopoly over interpretation?
7. Does a consent-first, graduated approach to offering help improve recipients' agency and reduce unintended harms compared with paternalistic intervention? Under what defined emergencies are exceptions justified?
8. Can access to essentials, security and opportunities for self-directed life improve without turning wellbeing metrics into compulsory definitions of happiness or human worth?

## Candidate study methods

- Comparative historical and institutional case studies with identified confounders.
- Tabletop exercises in which auditors can challenge founders, experts and emergency commanders.
- Agent-based or organisational simulations with declared assumptions and adversarial incentives; use them to explore possibilities, not to certify real-world morality.
- Participatory, rights-sensitive pilot processes in suitably bounded and voluntary settings.
- Independent review and transparent publication of failed as well as successful trials.

## Red-team scenarios

| Scenario | Question the safeguards must answer |
| --- | --- |
| Founder claims permanent immunity | Who can independently investigate and remove the founder? |
| Inspectors collude | Which outside actors retain evidence, appeal rights and authority to reopen findings? |
| Official hides a destructive error to protect social confidence | Can authentic evidence survive and later be recovered without unjustified exposure? |
| Protective force escalates after an initial provocation | Can intervention be bounded, proportionate and stopped? |
| Emergency persists for years without reassessment | What automatic expiry, independent renewal or rollback applies? |
| Two supposed options both cause severe harm | Who is required to seek a third option and record its feasibility? |
| A dramatic interpretation outruns verifiable evidence | Can coercive conclusions be suspended until the claim is appropriately substantiated? |
| Oversight itself silences criticism | Can the oversight body be appealed, reviewed and reconstituted? |
| An institution equates caring with unlimited authority | Can a person refuse offered help without punitive consequences where there is no lawful protective justification? |
| A programme celebrates rising wealth while basic needs remain unmet | Can the evaluation detect deprivation beneath favourable averages and impressive aggregate achievements? |
| An official success index ranks quiet or unconventional lives as failures | Can measures remain diagnostic without prescribing an acceptable lifestyle or emotional state? |
| Carers are praised but materially unsupported | Are responsibilities, rest, safety and voluntary support made realistically sustainable? |

## Potential measures and risks

Consider intervention-related injury, complaint accessibility, audit independence, time to correction, restored rights, confidentiality breaches, repeated failures, archived provenance integrity and the share of emergency measures that expire on schedule. Where relevant, examine reliable access to essentials, recipient-defined adequacy, opt-out accessibility, predictable support for carers, voluntary participation and inequalities hidden by averages. **Do not collapse these into a single virtue score.** Metrics become targets and may be gamed; pair quantitative reports with qualitative objections, victim testimony and independent checks.

## Adoption gates (design proposals)

1. Publish the proposed objective, known uncertainties and applicable rights constraints.
2. Invite external criticism and adversarial scenario testing.
3. Pilot reversibly when possible; do not test dangerous policies coercively on populations.
4. Demonstrate accessible appeal, review and withdrawal mechanisms before granting substantial authority.
5. Document both favourable and unfavourable outcomes, including failures to protect.
6. Revise or abandon a mechanism if evidence reveals systematic capture, unlawful harm or inability to correct mistakes.

## What success would *not* prove

Even a successful pilot would not establish universal benevolence, permanent institutional legitimacy, the impossibility of future abuse or a general licence for coercion. The [fallibility principle](FALLIBILITY_AND_CORRECTION.md) applies to the research programme itself.
