# Evidence, Interpretation and Civilisational Time

[← Index](README.md) · [Decisions under conflict](TRAGIC_CHOICES.md) · [Research and tests →](RESEARCH_AND_TESTS.md)

## A three-part orientation

**The past is evidence. The present is agency. The future is possibility.**

This is a philosophical orientation, not a claim that the past has no continuing effects or that future risks are unknowable. It proposes that institutions preserve what happened, act responsibly with information available now and keep the capacity to reconsider later outcomes.

## Maintain distinct epistemic categories

| Category | Meaning | Appropriate treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation or authenticated record | A trace of an event, measurement or testimony with provenance | Preserve originals and limitations |
| Interpretation | An account of what evidence means | Record author, assumptions and plausible alternatives |
| Hypothesis or forecast | A proposed causal account or future possibility | Specify tests, uncertainty and disconfirmation criteria |
| Normative judgement | A claim about what ought to matter or be permitted | Disclose values and affected interests; allow contestation |
| Decision | An action chosen under constraints and uncertainty | Record justification, alternatives, responsible authority and review path |

An intensely held interpretation need not be either disrespected or mistaken for independently verified evidence. A claim lacking accessible evidence cannot, by that absence alone, authorise coercive treatment of others.

## The preservation duty

The existing [civilisational memory framework](../MEMORY.md) requires original evidence to remain recoverable, with preservation, access and interpretation treated as separate duties. Apply that model to governance:

- Keep authentic records of decisions and effects, including mistakes and failures.
- Preserve dissenting evidence, audit trails, provenance and the history of corrections.
- Do not silently replace originals with corrected summaries.
- Where disclosure creates a specific credible danger or unjustified privacy loss, apply proportionate and reviewable access controls; preservation remains intact.
- Ensure archives survive institutional transition and loss of particular officials.

An institution must not claim benevolence as grounds for destroying proof of its own decisions. Protection of genuine confidences is compatible with retaining complete records for accountable examination.

## Evidence should discipline power

Before invoking a threat, a historical analogy or an emergency, distinguish:

1. What is directly known?
2. What is inferred and with what uncertainty?
3. Which independent sources support or challenge the inference?
4. What changes if that inference proves false?
5. What evidence would justify reducing or ending intervention?

## Temporal humility

Civilisations should avoid making permanent legal or cultural structures depend on predictions that cannot be challenged by later generations. They must preserve the resources and institutional freedoms required to revise earlier assumptions. The goal is continuity **with capacity for change**, not obedience to the past or unchecked confidence in a foreseen future.
