# Vision and Objectives

[← README](README.md) · [Ethics →](ETHICS.md)

## Central ambition

Seek conditions under which humanity and other life can **survive and continue evolving**, without an unavoidable terminal fate and without imposing stagnation. A recognisably human-descended entity should retain controlling or at least substantial influence over its own trajectory. What constitutes continuity of identity and legitimate influence is unresolved and requires collective examination.

This is a **normative ambition**, not a claim that indefinite survival is possible.

## Means are not ends

Technological progress, economic resources, dimensional expansion, knowledge, energy acquisition and political institutions are instruments. Their value depends on what they enable for living beings.

Accumulation for accumulation's sake, whether of money, territory, computational power or dimensions, is not the objective.

## Evolution and direction

'Forward' evolution is a provisional term. A fixed, universal definition of progress may be inappropriate. Different beings and future generations may value different forms of development. An evolving civilisation must be able to revise its own concepts of flourishing without erasing historical knowledge.

## Survival, continuity and quality of life

The framework distinguishes:

1. **Persistence:** some living or conscious lineage continues.
2. **Continuity:** its relationship to preceding humanity is meaningful, rather than a merely unrelated replacement.
3. **Agency:** descendants can influence their own conditions and decisions.
4. **Evolutionary capacity:** new forms of adaptation and development remain possible.
5. **Flourishing:** life remains capable of enjoyment, love, relationships, discovery and self-chosen meaning.

These aims can conflict. No simplistic formula should pretend otherwise.

## Provisional viability statement

For a possible future trajectory τ, define:

- H(τ,t): continued existence of a qualifying human-descended lineage;
- A(τ,t): meaningful agency;
- E(τ,t): capacity for further evolution.

An aspirational condition is:

```text
There exists a physically achievable trajectory τ such that
for every future time t:
    H(τ,t) holds,
    A(τ,t) remains meaningful,
    E(τ,t) remains possible.
```

This is **not a theorem**. 'Physically achievable', 'human-descended', 'meaningful' and 'evolution' all require operational definitions. A stronger theory would address resilience across many disturbances, not merely one fortunate trajectory.

Gödel's incompleteness theorems add a structural caution (hypothesis; the theorems themselves are stated in [MATHEMATICS.md](MATHEMATICS.md) section 9). Once the terms are defined inside a formal system rich enough to count configurations, there will be statements about the system's own trajectories it cannot settle; nor can it certify its own consistency. A viable civilisation therefore needs the capacity to extend its theory of itself, not merely to apply it. On this reading open-endedness is a requirement of the ambition rather than an ornament to it.

## What is not being proposed

- A prescribed purpose for all life.
- Compulsory immortality for every individual.
- A mandate to expand without limit.
- An obligation to maintain present human biology unchanged.
- A predetermined perfect society.
- A claim that current physics already permits escape from heat death.
- A complete and final theory of viability, which incompleteness makes unlikely in principle.
- An architect with unquestionable authority.
