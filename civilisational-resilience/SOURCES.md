# Sources and Evidence Boundaries

[← Adjunct index](README.md) · [Governance](GOVERNANCE_AND_RESEARCH.md)

The following sources support rights-based constraints and cautious assessment practice. They **do not** establish reincarnation, predetermined violent identity, a 'key individual' genetic class or a mathematically optimal distribution of people.

1. **UNFPA, *State of World Population 2023*: [Rights and choices are key](https://www.unfpa.org/swp2023/rights-choices).** Reproductive autonomy, freedom from discrimination, coercion and violence; demographic resilience as capacity to adapt rather than population quotas.
2. **UNICEF, [Convention on the Rights of the Child: treaty text](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text).** Especially Article 2 (non-discrimination), Article 3 (best interests), Articles 6 to 8 (life, development, name, identity) and Article 12 (participation).
3. **UNESCO, [Universal Declaration on Bioethics and Human Rights](https://www.unesco.org/en/ethics-science-technology/bioethics-and-human-rights).** Human dignity, informed consent, diversity and non-discrimination.
4. **World Health Organization (2021), [Guidance on community mental health services: promoting person-centred and rights-based approaches](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240025707/).** Model for rights-grounded, non-coercive community support in its domain.
5. **National Center for School Safety, [School Threat Assessment Toolkit, Section 2](https://www.nc2s.org/resource/school-threat-assessment-toolkit/section-2/).** Notes limitations of predictive profiles; assessment should focus on observable behaviour and context, with appropriate caveats for school settings.
6. **Original Evergence documents:** [ETHICS.md](../ETHICS.md), [MEMORY.md](../MEMORY.md), [ARCHITECTS.md](../ARCHITECTS.md), [RESEARCH.md](../RESEARCH.md), [ECONOMICS.md](../ECONOMICS.md). These are the framework's own prior commitments, not external empirical evidence.

## Claims not established

- That a particular prospective newborn has a known previous-life identity.
- That prior-life identity, heredity or ancestry validly predicts future violence.
- That selective breeding, assigned reproduction or coerced parent matching could safely improve civilisational resilience.
- That any class of people is morally more valuable or less deserving of freedom than another.

These claims must not be introduced silently through terminology such as 'fighter', 'breeding rights', 'key individuals', 'chosen births' or 'dangerous child'.
