# Civilisational Capability Distribution Without Eugenics

*A provisional Evergence research adjunct; not an assertion that people have predetermined social value or that prior-life identities can be empirically recognised.*

[← Extensions start](../START_HERE.md) · [Original Evergence](../README.md) · [Ethics](../ETHICS.md)

## The question

How can a civilisation prevent a community from being dangerously undersupplied with protective expertise, civic leadership, crisis response, education or care, **without** attempting to select who breeds, who is born, which families receive children or which individuals are considered intrinsically valuable or dangerous?

## Core distinction

**Distribute *capability and access*, not people by reproductive assignment.** People's moral worth is equal, irrespective of measured aptitude, heredity, geography, assumed destiny, disability or speculative claims about earlier lives. A community can broaden its access to expertise, care and emergency assistance without controlling its members' bodies or parenthood.

The proposal treats population distribution as a **service-coverage and institutional resilience** question, not a biological optimisation problem. Reproductive freedom and child welfare are prior constraints, not variables in an optimisation function.

## Scope and epistemic labels

- **Normative constraint:** No forced mating, breeding permits, reproduction quotas, sterilisation, assigned parenthood, adoption for strategic purposes, hereditary ranking, genetic-worth index or 'right to give birth to' a designated individual.
- **Evidence-grounded domain:** Regional coverage of training, health care, emergency response, education and institutional checks can be studied; administrative approaches have to be tested for effectiveness and side effects.
- **Speculative hypothetical:** Recognition of a specific returning person from a previous life and reliable prediction of a person's threat level based on that supposed identity, have not been scientifically established. These ideas cannot ground coercive action.

## Summary principle

> Civilisation should reduce the harm caused by uneven access to protective capability through education, voluntary cooperation, portable knowledge, redundant infrastructure and rights-respecting assistance, not by controlling reproduction or assigning people a destiny before birth.
