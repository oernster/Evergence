# Reproductive Autonomy, Equal Standing and Child Rights

[← Adjunct index](README.md) · [Capability distribution](CAPABILITY_DISTRIBUTION.md) · [Hypothetical](PRIOR_LIFE_HYPOTHETICAL.md)

## What this programme may never do

It must not establish or facilitate:

- Selection of 'fighter' couples for the purpose of producing a supposedly dangerous, valuable or predetermined person.
- Institutional 'birthing rights' over a particular imagined future consciousness or child; people are not assignable reproductive resources.
- Forced or pressured mating, insemination, pregnancy, sterilisation, contraception, embryo selection or reproductive quotas.
- Eugenic ranking of genes, ancestry, disability, intelligence, race, culture, sexuality, presumed fitness or familial worth.
- Removal or assignment of children based on a supposed prior-life identity, speculative threat inheritance or expected societal role.
- Heritable civic privileges or penalties; genetic data used as a stand-in for predicted criminality or moral character.
- Suppression of dissent, records or appeals concerning any of these practices.

These are non-negotiable **constraints**, not drawbacks to be balanced away against an extraordinary hypothetical threat.

## Legitimate voluntary pathways

- People can freely choose relationships and whether, when and with whom to have children, within the applicable rights and protections of law.
- Families may request education, parenting assistance, social care, counselling and community support, without being categorised as a reproductive instrument.
- Child protection decisions must centre the actual child's wellbeing, individual circumstances and due process, not ancestral, religious or speculative identity categories.
- Adult recruitment and training for emergency, protective or care roles may use transparent, relevant competency requirements, not hereditary desirability or reproductive status.

## Moral worth versus practical role

Civilisations may need firefighters, medical specialists, negotiators, researchers and dependable caregivers. A qualification for a particular job is **not** a moral rank. Uneven expertise is not unequal humanity. No failure to meet a proposed 'key person' threshold changes anyone's standing or liberty.

## Reference commitments

The [UNFPA statement on reproductive choice](https://www.unfpa.org/swp2023/rights-choices) stresses free decisions about childbearing without discrimination, coercion or violence. The [UN Convention on the Rights of the Child](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text), especially articles 2 and 3, supports non-discrimination and making children's best interests a primary consideration. [UNESCO's Universal Declaration on Bioethics and Human Rights](https://www.unesco.org/en/ethics-science-technology/bioethics-and-human-rights) sets out dignity, informed consent and non-discrimination principles. These authorities inform the proposal; they do not automatically resolve every legal dispute in every jurisdiction.

## Safeguard against 'benevolent' coercion

A programme cannot become non-eugenic merely by using kind or protective terminology. Inspect actual power relations: Who decides? Can participants refuse without penalty? Are children independent right-holders? Are coercive conditions hidden inside benefits or emergency declarations? Are decisions reviewable and reversible?

A body unable to demonstrate these protections must not make decisions concerning reproduction or family placement.
