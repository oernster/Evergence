# The Earlier-Life High-Threat Hypothetical: Boundaries and Responses

[← Adjunct index](README.md) · [Rights](RIGHTS_AND_REPRODUCTION.md) · [Research](GOVERNANCE_AND_RESEARCH.md)

## Precisely state the thought experiment

Suppose a future civilisation believes a particular person who once posed an extreme threat could reappear in another life. Suppose some citizens argue that exceptionally resilient prospective parents ('fighters', metaphorically) should acquire special 'birthing rights' in order to contain or raise that individual safely.

**Epistemic status:** speculative philosophical scenario. There is no scientifically established, independently reproducible method for assigning an actual child or prospective birth the identity of a particular earlier-life person or for predicting violence on that basis. A hypothetical must not be mistaken for an observed case.

## Two levels of analysis

**Under current knowledge:** no intervention predicated on the claimed earlier identity. No speculative labelling, surveillance, forced custody change, parent matching, reproduction policy or preventative punishment. Safeguarding responds to observable welfare needs and verifiable behaviour.

**If a future physical mechanism of personal continuity were independently established:** research might investigate what, if anything, persists between lives, including limits of identity, memory and prediction. Even then, identity would not establish that a new person is guilty of a predecessor's acts or fated to repeat them. The person's fundamental rights and the child's best interests remain primary. Parenting and custody rules would still be grounded in real caregiving and due process, not inherited culpability.

## What 'effective fighters' can mean safely

The productive interpretation is a **network of capable, compassionate responders and caregivers**, not an engineered breeding pair. Such people may offer voluntary mentorship, crisis response, welfare support, resilience training, conflict de-escalation, independent advocacy and protection of others from substantiated threats. They must never acquire reproductive rights over third parties.

## A non-eugenic response if someone becomes dangerous

1. Use **specific, current evidence** rather than a presumed identity, ancestry, prophecy or psychological profile.
2. Understand immediate safety needs, support needs and context; obtain appropriate multidisciplinary review.
3. Offer proportionate voluntary support wherever feasible; use any restriction only on lawful, necessary, reviewable grounds.
4. Protect potential victims and the person's procedural rights, privacy and access to support.
5. Reassess repeatedly rather than fixing someone permanently as a 'threat individual'.

The [National Center for School Safety threat assessment toolkit](https://www.nc2s.org/resource/school-threat-assessment-toolkit/section-2/) cautions against predictive profiles and notes that concern should be evaluated in context. This is a bounded source about school settings, not proof of a universal threat algorithm. [WHO's rights-based community services guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240025707/) reinforces support and protection of human dignity.

## Caution for Evergence

Narratives about predestined threats are especially susceptible to becoming their own destructive memeplex. A guardian institution might begin claiming unique access to previous-life identities, label dissenters as returning enemies and justify forced reproduction or separation of families. The antidote is evidence, refusal rights, independent challenge, robust archives and scrutiny of the institution itself.

**Research can preserve the hypothesis without empowering an authority to enact it.** See [Evergence's preservation principle](../MEMORY.md).
