# Sources and Evidentiary Notes

[← Extension index](README.md)

This is a selected research starting point, not a complete literature review. Primary historical evidence should be located and evaluated as part of the pilot; cited syntheses are not substitutes for original records. The extension's norms and hypotheses must not be presented as discoveries already demonstrated by these references.

## Cultural evolution and linguistic transmission

- Kirby, Simon; Griffiths, Tom; Smith, Kenny (2014). **“Iterated learning and the evolution of language.”** *Current Opinion in Neurobiology*, 28, 108 to 114. DOI [10.1016/j.conb.2014.07.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2014.07.014); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25062470/). **Supports:** cultural transmission and structure emerging through learning. **Does not establish:** a living autonomous language organism or direct causation of atrocities.

## Language and moral disengagement

- Bandura, Albert (1999). **“Moral disengagement in the perpetration of inhumanities.”** *Personality and Social Psychology Review*, 3(3), 193 to 209. DOI [10.1207/s15327957pspr0303_3](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327957pspr0303_3); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15661671/). **Supports:** sanitising language, diffusion of responsibility, dehumanisation and related social-cognitive processes. **Does not establish:** an intrinsic ethical status for any natural language.

## Nazi euphemisms and historical language

- United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, **[Holocaust Encyclopedia glossary](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/en/glossary)**. Includes descriptions of Nazi euphemisms such as *Sonderbehandlung* and 'Final Solution'.
- USHMM, **[“Combating Holocaust Denial: Origins of Holocaust Denial”](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/combating-holocaust-denial-origins-of-holocaust-denial)**. Explains the concealment of killing through euphemism and coded descriptions.
- USHMM, **[“Wannsee Conference and the ‘Final Solution’”](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/wannsee-conference-and-the-final-solution)**. Places euphemisms in a documented administrative and historical setting.
- Klemperer, Victor (German publication history varies by edition; English edition **[The Language of the Third Reich: LTI](https://www.bloomsbury.com/uk/language-of-the-third-reich-9781472507211/)**). A major historical analysis of the language of Nazism. *Bibliographic note:* consult the relevant edition and its publication history when citing pages; the linked English edition is a later translation and printing.

## Language heritage and rights

- UNESCO, **[Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/convention)** (2003), especially Article 2, on oral traditions and expressions, including language as a vehicle of intangible cultural heritage.
- OHCHR, **[Human Rights and Constitution Making](https://www.ohchr.org/Documents/Publications/ConstitutionMaking_EN.pdf)**, quoting Article 27 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights: linguistic minorities' right to use their own language.
- United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (2007), Articles 13 and 14, on the right to use, develop, revitalise and transmit languages; text reproduced in an official Canadian legislative resource [here](https://lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/U-2.2/FullText.html).

## Explicit gaps

Further work is required to examine: cultural evolutionary models beyond iterated learning; critique of memetics; cross-linguistic pragmatics and euphemism; comparative propaganda research; signed-language documentation; informed consent for community archives; archive security and sustainability; and causal evidence for language intervention efficacy. No settled conclusion about compulsory retirement of an entire language follows from the sources above.
