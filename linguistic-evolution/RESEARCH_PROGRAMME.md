# Research Programme: Test Linguistic Risk Without Predetermining Outcomes

[← Extension index](README.md) · [German case study](GERMAN_CASE_STUDY.md) · [Sources →](SOURCES.md)

## Fit with Evergence

This is a proposed **additional workstream** subordinate to the evidence and reporting conventions of [RESEARCH.md](../RESEARCH.md), its milestone M4 for a historical archive prototype and its existing red-team question about archival controls becoming censorship. It does not edit the repository's original workstreams or assert completion of any research.

## Research questions

**R1 (Replication):** Under what conditions do morally consequential expressions, frames or labels spread and persist? Can transmission be distinguished from independent invention or translation?

**R2 (Compression):** Which kinds of shorthand, euphemism or conceptual packaging change comprehension, perceived responsibility or moral salience? When do they *increase* clarity instead?

**R3 (Institutions):** Do observed effects remain after accounting for propaganda, censorship, class, religious belief, political incentives, policy, threat and coercive authority?

**R4 (Cross-language transfer):** Do comparable contextual mechanisms appear across unrelated languages, including signed and minority languages? What differences emerge under careful matching?

**R5 (Intervention):** Do education, transparent definitions, clearer administrative wording and participatory review reduce harm without suppressing rights or evidence?

**R6 (Self-capture):** How does a linguistic-risk assessor evolve its own jargon, reward systems and incentives? Can it be empirically shown to resist political capture?

## Methods

- Build **consent-aware, provenance-rich multilingual corpora**, including non-text modalities where feasible; sample normal language as well as propaganda and institutional language to prevent selection bias.
- Use historical process tracing with primary documents and source criticism, not just retrospective word-frequency analysis.
- Pre-register hypotheses, comparison groups, annotation standards and outcome measures before selecting attractive examples.
- Use qualified multilingual scholars and community representatives for interpretation and translation; publish ambiguity instead of forcing a single gloss.
- Conduct ethically approved, low-risk comprehension studies with opt-in participants and debriefing; do not reproduce propaganda for persuasion.
- Triangulate observational results against matched comparisons, temporal order, alternative causal explanations and independent replication.
- Evaluate false positives/false negatives, especially where minority languages are underrepresented; document distribution shifts over time.
- Test institutional language used by **the investigators themselves** under the same scrutiny.
- Require each investigator to declare, with the study and before its findings, any affective attachment to or against a language under study and to the history of its speakers; record it as a confounder beside the data, since such feeling attaches itself to surface features of a language and makes them look like evidence (see the politeness convention in the [German case study](GERMAN_CASE_STUDY.md)).

## Observable outputs, not a universal ranking

Candidate outputs include replication/retention patterns for expressions, documented lexical changes, comprehension differences in controlled studies, policy records showing changes of usage and audit outcomes. Interpret each with uncertainty, plausible confounders and rights impacts.

Do **not** produce a single civilisational ranking of languages as morally superior or inferior. No unvalidated classifier is a legitimate basis for restricting a language.

## Pilot milestones

| Pilot | Required deliverable | Failure or halt condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1: Terminology | Scope, definitions, rights charter, contested alternatives | Terms fail community review or conflate language with ethnicity |
| P2: Historical sources | Source-indexed Nazi euphemism case with original context and counterexamples | Inadequate provenance, anachronistic translation, excluded testimony |
| P3: Comparison | Two or more further cross-language case studies using identical analytic standards | No credible matched comparator; opportunistic selection |
| P4: Mechanism | Preregistered tests of whether wording changes interpretation or choices | Null/contradictory results unreported or unsupported causal claim |
| P5: Archive | Restoration, provenance, appeal and translation demonstrations | Source becomes inaccessible or annotations overwrite originals |
| P6: Governance | Independent capture tests, dissent publication, review and sunset exercise | Assessor can suppress scrutiny, revoke language rights or evade appeal |

No fixed schedule or automatic trigger converts these milestones into a coercive programme. Phase completion authorises scrutiny of the next phase, not the next restriction.

## Falsification and stopping criteria

A mechanism claim should be abandoned or substantially revised when well-powered replications fail, confounders explain the observations, comparative cases contradict universality or its proposed interventions produce more harm than credible benefit. If the rights charter or archive-preservation requirements cannot be honoured, stop intervention work and preserve the research record.

## Required public record

For each claim: epistemic status; publication date; researchers and conflicts; original data provenance where lawful; preregistration; methods; uncertainties; strongest objections; counterexamples; intervention alternatives; affected stakeholders; whether the proposal was rejected or revised; and accessible appeal/contact information.

## Red-team scenarios

Test a state that labels all opposition 'linguistically harmful'; an assessor pressured to favour a dominant language; AI mistranslations weaponised against minorities; an 'archive' used as a pretext to stop teaching a language; historical records silently cleaned; and public misinformation that attributes two world wars to the grammatical structure of German.

**Success means improved understanding and reduced harm while preserving knowledge and agency, not the disappearance of any language.**
