# Archival Stewardship: Preserve, Recover, Contextualise

[← Extension index](README.md) · [Universal model](UNIVERSAL_MODEL.md) · [Governance safeguards →](GOVERNANCE_SAFEGUARDS.md)

## Relationship to Evergence's existing archival rules

[MEMORY.md](../MEMORY.md) is authoritative for Evergence's three duties of **preservation**, **access** and **interpretation**, as well as distributed custody, provenance, integrity and access appeals. This document adds a language-specific profile. Nothing here authorises destruction, silent rewriting, politically convenient erasure or the permanent loss of contested accounts.

**Normative invariant:** no designated language archive should erase the original language, authentic historical sources or the ability of future communities to recover and reevaluate them, insofar as physically feasible. This remains a constrained engineering aspiration rather than a promise of perfect preservation.

## Four separate statuses, not one euphemistic 'archive' label

| Status | Meaning | Effect on use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Preserved and living** | Recorded and actively spoken, signed, written or otherwise transmitted | No restriction implied |
| **Historically contextualised** | Specific usages or periods catalogued as requiring interpretation or warning | No restriction implied |
| **Institutionally deprecated expression** | A particular term is discouraged or replaced in a defined organisational setting | Only the stated institutional practice changes; historical original remains accessible |
| **Dormant or historical language** | A language is no longer used routinely by a community, often following historical change or community choice | Revival and learning remain possible and legitimate |

These statuses must not be treated as a scale on which languages automatically progress toward suppression. A **compulsory retirement** is a distinct, coercive action that the present model neither recommends nor legitimates.

## Minimum archival record

A serious linguistic archive would aim to retain, as appropriate and with community participation:

- Original inscriptions, books, newspapers, letters, digital texts and version histories, with accurate provenance.
- Natural speech and oral history recordings, pronunciation, intonation, prosody and listening contexts.
- Signed-language video and relevant embodied/visual features rather than text translations alone.
- Lexicons, grammars, morphology, syntax, dialect maps, semantic histories and loanword records.
- Historical media, educational materials, art, song, humour and everyday interactions, not only exceptional or harmful material.
- Contextual annotations and contradictory interpretations, stored separately from the primary record.
- Source-to-translation relationships with documented uncertainty; terminology for which translation is disputed.
- Rights, consent, privacy and culturally sensitive access agreements, including the interests of vulnerable communities and victims.
- Means for retraining readers, speakers, signers and future interpreters, where possible, so preservation is recoverable knowledge rather than uninterpretable bytes.

A text-only archive is not a complete archive of human language. A complete historic linguistic record is not physically attainable, so losses and sampling limitations must be documented honestly.

## Operational integrity and resilience

Recommended mechanisms: cryptographic checksums; auditable provenance; redundant geographically and institutionally distributed custody; open and migratable formats; periodic restoration tests; independent custodians; maintained metadata; a published inventory of known losses; and no destructive 'correction' of an original item. These implement, rather than replace, [MEMORY.md](../MEMORY.md).

Retention priorities require transparent budgets and consultation with affected communities; [ECONOMICS.md](../ECONOMICS.md) already recognises memory as a scarce and morally significant resource.

## Access, context and safety

Public scholarly access is the default aspiration, including access for the communities concerned. Where a **specific item** presents credible operational danger, privacy harms or victim-protection concerns, the narrowly tailored access conditions in [MEMORY.md](../MEMORY.md) apply. Mere political embarrassment, criticism or a historically uncomfortable argument is not grounds for restriction. Every restriction needs a recorded basis, appeal and review date.

Sensitive materials may require trauma-informed explanation or controls over personally identifying information. Historical originals must remain identifiable as originals; commentary, educational redactions and translations must be separately versioned. Public explanations should not falsely imply that an annotated text is a verbatim source.

## Revival, dignity and participation

Communities retain the right to teach, develop and transmit their own languages. Preserving a living language should support, not undermine, its present and future speakers. UNESCO's intangible-heritage framework and international instruments concerning linguistic minorities and indigenous peoples are relevant rights baselines (see [Sources](SOURCES.md)).

**Archive preservation and living linguistic freedom must reinforce one another**, not become alternatives imposed by outside authorities.

## Archive acceptance tests

The system should demonstrate that a third-party custodian can restore data; a reader can distinguish source from annotation; competing interpretations remain intact; controlled material has a reviewable access path; and authorised community representatives can challenge misdescription, mistranslation or an inappropriate restriction.
