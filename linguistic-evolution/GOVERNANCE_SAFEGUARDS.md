# Linguistic Risk Governance Without Linguistic Authoritarianism

[← Extension index](README.md) · [Archival stewardship](ARCHIVAL_STEWARDSHIP.md) · [German case study →](GERMAN_CASE_STUDY.md)

## Principal danger: the guardians becoming the mechanism

**Normative.** No institution may claim that protecting civilisation from harmful language gives it unlimited power to classify speech, monopolise interpretation, suppress a living language or erase contrary evidence. The judgement 'dangerous language' can itself become a compact, self-replicating political category. The institution must therefore be subject to the same critique it applies to others.

This specialises (it does not replace) the anti-coercion provisions of [ETHICS.md](../ETHICS.md), the accountability provisions of [ARCHITECTS.md](../ARCHITECTS.md) and the provenancing/access provisions of [MEMORY.md](../MEMORY.md).

## Rights and jurisdiction

- Protect linguistic and cultural expression, education, translation, scholarship, artistic speech, dissent, minority-language use and revival.
- Distinguish **speech analysis** from ordinary lawful enforcement against threats, harassment, incitement or unlawful conduct; never conflate the two automatically.
- Preserve a meaningful right to criticise the assessment body, contest its labels and publish alternative analyses.
- Include affected speakers and communities in design and review; especially account for asymmetries of power.
- Recognise differences among legal jurisdictions and do not pretend this proposal constitutes a universal binding legal code.

In particular, *German* as a living language is not a legitimate stand-in for *Nazi terminology or institutional propaganda*. Identical standards apply to all language communities.

## Reviewable decision procedure

1. **Notice and specific scope.** Identify the expression, context and institution; avoid global labels such as 'this language is dangerous'.
2. **Public hypothesis and evidence.** State proposed mechanism, evidence type, uncertainties, rival explanations and conflicts of interest.
3. **Community participation.** Obtain meaningful input from affected speakers, linguists, historians, rights experts, archivists and potentially harmed groups; accommodate minority viewpoints.
4. **Proportionality test.** Assess harm and causal confidence against education, contextualisation and other less restrictive alternatives.
5. **Independent adjudication.** The investigative body must not have final unreviewable decision-making power; separation of powers and outside audit are essential.
6. **Published reasons and appeal.** Record competing opinions, rights implications, dissent, access rules, review dates and means to reverse decisions.
7. **Reassessment and sunset.** Test whether any intervention works without introducing greater harms; withdraw or revise when evidence or legitimacy fails.

**Stop rule:** if independent oversight, procedural fairness, community rights or preservation cannot be maintained, no coercive intervention may proceed. An urgent claim alone does not suspend that rule.

## Avoiding a misleading score

No numerical 'language danger index' should become a policy target. It would risk Goodhart-style gaming, spurious precision, misclassification and discrimination, exactly the incentive failures already considered in [ECONOMICS.md](../ECONOMICS.md). Diagnostic indicators may be researched; they must never automatically trigger restrictions.

A proposed model must undergo independent validation across languages, dialects, modalities, historical periods and political contexts. AI classifiers, where used, are aids for *research triage*, not judges of permissible thought or speech.

## Adversarial safeguards

Require a standing red-team mandate to test:

- **Capture:** an administration defines criticism of itself as 'dangerous language'.
- **Discrimination:** one nation, ethnic group or minority language is disproportionately investigated.
- **Manufactured evidence:** state-controlled corpora or translations falsely demonstrate risk.
- **Mission creep:** an archive classification becomes an employment, education or citizenship restriction.
- **False negatives:** reassuring euphemisms conceal ongoing harm even after a label is applied.
- **Institutional self-exemption:** assessors forbid scrutiny of their own terms, metrics or procedures.
- **Irreversibility:** materials or skills are lost after a decision presented as simple archiving.

Record these findings publicly, subject only to justified protections for individuals and genuinely dangerous operational specifics.

## Default practical recommendation

Use evidence-based teaching, transparent public-administration language, independent archives, interdisciplinary scholarship, translation literacy, victim-centred documentation and open historical debate. Investigate and correct harmful *uses* and *institutions* first. Whole-language restrictions are neither scientifically substantiated nor part of the proposed intervention set.
